# 6DOF mechanics, evidence and calibration limits

The first planar/scalar-tilt prototype was rejected as insufficient. This revision replaces its mechanics, not just its effects. **It is still an uncalibrated rigid-body approximation, not a verified digital twin.** Manufacturer ratings remain factual catalog values. The current part model uses them, together with source-observed shape and mechanism descriptions, to constrain explicitly estimated geometry, inertia, contact loss and retention parameters. This is a disclosed game-model hypothesis, not a manufacturer SI conversion or an experimentally established relationship.

## Rigid-body state and integration

Each top has world COM position/velocity in three dimensions, a unit quaternion, world angular momentum, body inertia, and derived angular velocity `ω = R I_body⁻¹ Rᵀ L`. Gravity accelerates the COM vertically. Contact impulses change both linear momentum and angular momentum `ΔL = r × J`; actual support torque produces precession and lean. No prescribed tilt target, decorative wobble, hidden winner selection or speed multiplier is used.

The symmetric-top torque-free propagator preserves world angular momentum and rotational energy; asymmetric body inertia uses an iterative Lie midpoint update. Fixed public steps are 1/240 s with deterministic internal substeps limiting angular feature travel and translation. The quaternion, momenta, contact state and finish state are JSON-serializable. The authoritative server sends that same state to both clients.

## Contact and Xtreme Dash

* The bowl is a curved height field with its matching surface normal. Flat tips use a finite patch, while rounded/needle/taper proxies use rounded contact with a declared effective torsional patch radius. Coulomb slip, rolling resistance and body-floor contacts transfer/dissipate energy. Tilt changes the real contact position.
* Lobed blade geometry is transformed by the actual quaternion. Rounded-node contact proxies calculate three-dimensional off-center impulses. The visible outline comes from the shared geometry export, but the rounded contact manifold is **not exact CAD collision detection**.
* The rail is seated in a continuous estimated molded shoulder, rather than left as a free-standing fence above a traversable outer trough. The same floor/bank/ramp surface and analytic normal drive contacts and rendering. See the [reproduced rail-trap repair](rail-regression.md), including the rejected over-high shoulder and fixed assembly clearances.
* The low gear rail and the outer, higher cover wall are separate surfaces. Finite 3D gear/rail solid contact is independent of ideal meshing eligibility; only real inner radial side contact may mesh, never axial/back-side support or separating proximity. Normal support and gear coupling use the same contact arm in a unilateral coupled solve; a vertically re-entering gear contacts the rail edge rather than being snapped laterally when mesh criteria become true. The blade can overhang the bit's rail; an old single-radius wall clamp must not prevent the gear reaching the rail.
* The closed rail follows the official stadium's approximate topology: a rear inward notch and front Xtreme Zone flanked by two Over Zones. Gear contact constrains the tangential contact velocity using the actual transformed bit pitch radius and full effective contact mass. It converts spin energy into travel; release from the notch follows the contact geometry, not a scripted inward steering force. This is smooth pitch contact, not individually resolved gear-tooth phase/impact; visible teeth are an illustrative geometric proxy.
* Events distinguish gear engagement, release, an actual inward-travelling dash, collision and finish. Dash telemetry uses signed net translational KE gain and full rotational KE loss immediately across gear impulses, plus inward release and at least one modeled tooth pitch of travel; it does not add forces or treat scalar-spin change as energy. UI speed, lean, traces and rail contact markers read solver state. A counted event alone is not proof of correct real-world behavior.

## How official performance now affects the solver

The earlier revision ignored ratings in physical parameters; that did not satisfy the requested part-dependent game. The replacement uses raw published scores divided by 100, not normalization to a sum of 100 (which would erase metal-coat differences). Missing scores retain explicit missing-input metadata and a declared fallback prior, never a fabricated official zero.

- Blade attack/defense constrain the estimated protrusion, edge sharpness and thickness. Source descriptions choose asymmetric uppercut, downward smash, single-edge, swept-wing or rounded forms. Stamina and observed outer-weight construction constrain mass distribution and axial inertia.
- Ratchet protrusion count, height class and source-described asymmetry change lower contact geometry, COM and exposure. Conditional left/right ratings do not change the same physical ratchet's mass; they constrain a fixed asymmetric outline. Height code 60 is **not** asserted to equal a measured 6 mm.
- Bit shape and attack/defense ratings constrain tip contact radius and friction. Stamina constrains rolling and spin losses. Dash ratings and observed gear geometry constrain pitch/contact coupling; Rush's 10-tooth form and extended-gear families are distinct. Energy still comes from spin and launch, not a dash multiplier.
- Burst resistance and ratchet defense constrain a directional detent-energy barrier. Published simple-joint conditions are applied separately from the bit's unchanged catalog rating. No score is applied as direct opponent damage or winner selection.
- Restitution remains a common estimated material constant rather than increasing arbitrarily with attack. The exact bounded component masses listed in [independent mass observations](independent-mass-observations.md) use selected independently reported specimen readings, not manufacturer specifications or certified calibration. Unmatched masses, all dimensions, density distributions, COM/inertia, friction and locking constants remain estimates. Verified relative height differences are identified separately in the inspector.

The same power setting supplies the same estimated initial rotational and translational work, not the same RPM regardless of inertia: `E_rot = 0.5 × I_ref × (170 + 440p)^2`, `ω = sqrt(2E_rot/I_axial)`, with `I_ref = 0.55 × 0.035 × 0.024²`. Translational work is `0.5 × 0.035 × ((0.45 + 1.45p)^2 + 0.12^2)`, including the small downward release velocity. Initial gravitational potential can differ with geometry and is not launcher work. These launcher laws are also estimates, not measured pull-string mechanics.

Metadata exposes each selected component's official values, source, missing inputs, unsupported mechanisms and derived estimates. Full descriptions and source URLs stay outside authoritative per-frame snapshots. Unknown shapes are explicitly marked **形状未再現・暫定モデル**. Source-informed does not mean exact CAD: blade bevel dimensions, ratchet teeth, palettes and contact manifolds remain approximations. Free bearings, transforming bits and dampers are not silently claimed as independent simulated mechanisms. Unknown factual physical parameters remain `null` in downloadable official catalog data.

## Finish rules and limitations

The official Japanese [12th edition rules](https://beyblade.takaratomy.co.jp/beyblade-x/_image/regulation.pdf), effective 2026-03-13, specify Spin 1, Over 2, Burst 2 and Xtreme 3 points, first occurrence priority and simultaneous relaunches. Default play is two players, one bey, first to at least four points. Pocket catch surfaces are lower than the playing bowl, with gravity-driven entry and estimated containment. Whole-body zone entry is provisional while a return remains possible. Positive low RPM or lean alone does not count as Spin Finish.

Burst uses exposed lower-ratchet contacts and a directional quasistatic detent-energy estimate; generic blade impact is not accumulated as arbitrary damage. Ordinary supported assemblies now separate into the assembled blade unit, ratchet and bit at release. CX blade internals remain one blade body, not an invented explosion of every layer. Initial component velocities are inherited as `v + ω × r`; a positive inertia/parallel-axis partition preserves total mass, linear momentum, world angular momentum and kinetic energy. Component centers and tensors come from the same geometry/density integration as the intact assembly; no spacing is fitted afterward to force a diagonal inertia. The densities and geometry remain estimates, not measured component properties. Stored detent energy is dissipated, not converted into a random kick. Identical shared component geometry preserves all initial world positions at the split.

The result is latched once. Both the surviving assembly and detached bodies continue gravity, floor/body/containment contacts for a bounded 2.5-second **unscored observation window**. No score or finish is recalculated. A piece may still be spinning when the observation ends; no stronger fake damping forces it to stop. Both clients receive the same fragment poses, and next-round input is gated until the window ends. Integrated-ratchet/integrated-bit structures without a modeled release mechanism retain an explicit unsupported warning and do not fabricate three detachable pieces. Product-specific transformations, Bullet Griffon's separate bullet, internal free-spinning parts, exact blade material/contact deformation, launcher mechanics and every catalog part's shape are not reproduced.

## Verification and reproducible trajectories

The checked-in controlled-comparison reports were generated on Node 24.22.1; their exact trajectories and outcomes are runtime-specific. Replay determinism is scoped to the same engine/runtime version, platform, source, numeric profile and serialized initial state. It is **not** a bitwise cross-V8/browser guarantee. Geometry/mass integration and contact math use native floating-point transcendental functions; tiny differences can grow through repeated contacts and change the finish time or winner. On revision `53f7d88`, the same default input closed at 17.875 simulated seconds on Node 22.23.3 and 12.804 seconds on Node 24.22.1, both with two real dashes and no positive energy tick. Initial differences were approximately 1e−20 kg·m² in tensors, 1e−18 m in COM and 1e−14 rad/s in angular velocity, not different selected parts, specimen masses or geometric dimensions. Tests therefore retain physical invariants and a bounded observation window rather than selecting a cross-runtime winner. Online play remains server-authoritative: clients receive the same server-generated poses and result instead of independently deciding the outcome.

- Unit tests check torque-free momentum/energy, quaternion normalization, gravity/support torque, precession, off-center contact, gear energy transfer, shared geometry, deterministic replay and finish arbitration. These establish software/physics invariants, **not measured equivalence**.
- `npm run analyze:beyblade -- /tmp/beyblade-trajectories.json` runs the public-default matchup, its previous-input regression, plus twelve ordinary launch cases across the four starter forms. It records actual positions/orientations, energy, speed, lean, gear/release/collision events, round outcome and the core source SHA-256. It never injects special positions or velocities to manufacture a dash.
- Browser tests verify real two-client authoritative quaternion/momentum snapshots, customization, interrupted flows, phone layout and online-unavailable behavior.

Before claiming faithful reproduction: obtain measured mass/inertia/geometry and launch distributions; fit contact and gear parameters against high-speed trajectories; compare held-out matchups, rail timing/speed, precession, rebound, finish rates and uncertainty. Public video frames without camera calibration are qualitative evidence, not millimetre/second ground truth.

The current editable launch preset is 60% / 0° / 20°. It was selected from ordinary legal inputs to show the mechanism without scripted forces; the previous 80% / 0° / 8° input remains a regression fixture and can legitimately self-exit. No build-specific demo force or winner rule is used. (Preset history: the original 60% / +15° / 20° trajectory closed at 12.8 s with two dashes before the 2026-10-31 recalibration below; afterwards it no longer finished within 30 s, so the preset was reselected at the same power/tilt with a 0° aim, which shows gearing, dashes and an Xtreme finish in ~8 s.)

## Duration and round-to-round variation recalibration (2026-10-31)

User feedback: rounds were far shorter than the physical game and identical inputs replayed identically. Measured references gathered for this recalibration (community measurement sites and video-verified match data, not manufacturer SI data): real combinations spin solo for roughly 60–170 s depending on bit (Ball longest, Flat ~60–90 s); real rounds typically finish in ~17–60 s with early Over/Xtreme finishes in the first ~3–10 s. The previous build's 14-case trajectory report had a 3.9 s median finish, bursts at 0.5–0.9 s from a single contact, and a 20° launch preset losing ~58% of its spin in the first second.

Changes, all to declared estimate parameters (no scripted forces, no winner selection):

- Blade/ratchet floor-scrape friction .28/.25 → .10/.09 and smaller effective tip torsional patch radii: the dominant early spin drain was scrape/patch loss at ordinary launch tilts, not the spin-drag term.
- Spin-loss (`drag`) range .010–.025 → .007–.017 /s.
- Blade-to-blade friction cap .25 → .07 of the normal impulse: one clash previously cancelled most relative surface slip and erased up to ~80% of a top's spin; real metal clashes are brief and elastic. Restitution .48 → .38.
- Burst detent barrier raised ~1.4× (stiffness/release angle) and only 40% of a ratchet contact's dissipated energy now twists the detent (the rest is heat/sound/deformation), so bursts build over repeated strong hits instead of one first contact.
- Positional floor correction now applies only to approaching contacts and is capped at 2 mm; separating (wobbling) vertices no longer gain potential from correction alone. The full-stadium energy tests use the declared correction budget of mass·g·1 mm per body per tick plus the same bound cumulatively, as documented above.
- Launch micro-variation: `createBattle` accepts an optional seed (default 0 = exact legacy inputs, which the compatibility tests pin). A non-zero seed samples power ±3%, angle ±1°, tilt ±0.8°, default-spawn position ±2 mm and a uniform yaw phase once at creation and bakes them into the initial state; stepping, snapshots, reconnects and settling remain deterministic for that seed. A player-chosen launch position is never jittered. Local play draws a fresh seed per round (a `?seed=N` URL parameter pins it for replay/tests); the authoritative server draws per round through an injectable `launchSeed` option.

After the change, the 14-case trajectory report's median finish is 6.2 s (was 3.9 s) and the public-default case lasts 33.5 s by Spin; over 20 seeds the default matchup's median is ~6 s with a long right tail (mean ~12 s, max 50 s). This is closer to, but still shorter and more variable than, the measured real distribution: sub-second finishes still occur in a few seeded spawn clashes, and two of the fourteen cases (stamina builds at low tilt) now orbit past the report's 40 s cap without contact. Solo spin in the floor-only fixture is ~54–150 s for Taper/Ball builds but still short for Flat (~19 s upright), so the tip-patch model remains the least calibrated part. This recalibration is tuned toward measured durations; it is **not** a measured calibration and does not claim real-world equivalence.

Contact stabilization is still a numerical approximation. The pre-bank full-tensor revision recorded a +0.461 mJ correction tick in an off-default regression while total energy remained below launch energy; this is historical evidence, not a guarantee about the revised bank. The exact reported rail-trap input after the bank fix has no positive-energy ticks in its recorded trace. Full-stadium tests track a declared mass × gravity × 1 mm potential-correction budget separately from stricter free-motion/contact invariants. No energy cap hides this error. Wider parameter sweeps and continuous-contact refinement remain necessary; this is not proof of perfect dissipation for all estimated geometries.

## Round 2: longer battles, burst reachability and CX customization (2026-10-31)

User feedback on the round-1 build: battles still felt short and fast-forwarded, finishes clustered in the first seconds, and bursts had effectively disappeared from ordinary matchups. Traced causes and the changes made, all to declared estimate parameters or solver bounds (no scripted forces, no winner selection):

- One `resolveContact` call could dissipate 1.15 J — the pair's whole spin budget — because the rigid solve sees blade-tip surface speeds near 15 m/s. A single-strike normal-impulse cap of 0.06 N·s (ordinary clashes measure ~0.007 N·s; the pathological call measured 0.22 N·s) bounds one substep to a glancing, patch-limited strike.
- The deepest node pair can be a high blade node against a low bit node, producing a near-vertical contact normal and a full-body vertical impulse (traced tops airborne at y = 0.23 m, above the 0.16 m cover roof). The contact normal's vertical component is now clamped to |n.y| ≤ 0.5 (30°).
- Rail engagement drained ~75% of a top's spin per engagement in traces. Gear meshing now stops once a top's spin falls below 70% of its engagement-start spin; the rail can still accelerate translation, it just cannot strip a nearly-spent top.
- Blade-to-blade friction cap .07 → .05 of the normal impulse and restitution .38 → .34: clashes stay brief and elastic.
- Burst detent, reworked after measurement showed the round-1 stack had made bursts unreachable (0 bursts in 48 measured rounds, and zero ratchet-node deepest pairs in a traced 30 s battle — blades always overlap deeper):
  - Lock loading now accepts strikes landing at ratchet level: an exposed ratchet node, or any contact point within the victim's ratchet band height (ratchet half-thickness + 8 mm). High blade hits above the band still never load the detent.
  - The per-event dissipation gate was removed after tracing showed per-call dissipation is usually microjoules in this solver (one call in a 30 s battle exceeded 0.15 mJ); accumulation across band strikes is the intended path.
  - Dissipated fraction entering the detent .22 → .35; per-strike twist cap 50% of the release angle (one hit alone can never burst); detent barrier ~2.2× the original (stiffness/release angle in `physical-estimates.ts`); twist decay slowed from τ ≈ 4 s to τ ≈ 60 s so loosening accumulates across a round instead of healing between clashes.
- The local client's post-finish settling now advances at 0.35× real time (XONE-style dramatic finish, presentation only); the battle simulation itself is unchanged.

Measured after the change (12 seeds per matchup, mirror launches {power .76, angle −7°, tilt 6°} with per-seed micro-variation): vs DranSword median 29.9 s (range 1.2–61 s, 11 Spin / 1 Over); vs WizardArrow median 14.8 s (range 0.7–68.7 s, finishes mixed incl. 1 Burst); mirror median 2.5 s. Bursts occur naturally (5 of 36 battles) but remain uncommon. Known limits, unchanged in kind from round 1: an early-finish cluster persists in some matchups (~30–50% under 5 s against the stamina build and in mirror launches, mostly dash-related Over/Xtreme/draw outcomes; a rare ~0.7 s airborne double exit was traced but its full cause was not isolated), and solo Flat spin (~19 s) is still far below the real 60–90 s. This remains a tuned estimate, **not** a measured calibration and not real-world equivalence.

Test fixtures: outcomes in the 240 Hz solver are chaotic, and the shared Hells burst fixture was knife-edge (it burst locally but spun in CI on the round-1 build, and stopped bursting entirely after this recalibration). Burst-dependent tests (`burst.test.ts`, `multiplayer.test.ts`) now deterministically select the first pair from a fixed list of server-legal launches (power .2–1, tilt ≤ 0.65 rad) that produces a genuine burst in the same deterministic physics, then assert on that real battle exactly as before; if no pair bursts the test fails, so burst reachability stays covered. The Rod/5-70/DB fixture moved to power .8 with a 30 s observation loop (Spin finish ~15.9 s, radius .219 m, gear mesh intact) and the rail-bank Sword/N fixture to power .5 (opponent driven exterior and back to .021 m, Spin ~24 s). No assertion was loosened; the deterministic-replay test gained an explicit timeout matching its sibling trajectory tests because 1000 solver steps exceed the 5 s default under parallel workers.

CX customization: assist blades and over blades are now freely swappable in the builder (the other CX slots stay restricted to source-verified assemblies). A swapped assist/over part shifts the assembled blade's official attack/defense/stamina ratings by the difference between the chosen and stock parts where official ratings exist; where they do not (assist/over parts carry no official ratings), a documented design-intent trait table by part letter is used and labelled as an estimate. Stock assemblies are numerically unchanged. Bey/part lists are sorted with `Intl.Collator('ja')`.

## Round 3: dished stadium, center gathering and duration (2026-10-31)

User feedback on the round-2 build: the stadium did not read as dished and beys did not gather at the center, rounds still felt short, and the opponent's parts could not be chosen. The stadium and duration changes:

- Bowl depth .012 → .020 m with a reshaped profile: parabolic through the middle (u = r²/R² < .55), then the slope scale eases to a residual 50% toward the rail lane. Measured before: a stamina top orbited at r .11–.16 m for 40+ s without converging (the 12 mm dish was too weak and planar orbital motion was effectively undamped). After: the same mirror stamina launches spiral from r .113 to ~.04–.08 m over 40 s. The residual outer slope keeps a top knocked to the wall draining back instead of parking on a flat ring, and keeps blade/ratchet clearance for an upright rail-meshed top (measured +1.1 mm minimum; the strict-parabola variant failed that clearance at −0.3 mm and a fully flat outer lane parked a top at r .211 for a whole round). The rail path height now derives from the same bowl function (previously a hardcoded parabola), and the rendered floor already samples `bowlHeightAt`, so sight and physics agree.
- Translational rolling resistance at the tip: while the tip carries load, planar momentum bleeds in proportion to the normal impulse (~0.04 m/s² at rest load). Tip friction alone opposes total slip, which the spin surface velocity dominates, so orbits never decayed. Impulse-proportional form keeps it dt-free for the direct `resolveFloor` test callers.
- Translational launch speed × .92 (spin speed unchanged) and restitution .34 → .30: full legacy speed against the center-gathering bowl still produced first-seconds pocket ejections.
- Detent rework (later the same day, after the first shipped round-3 build measured a 9.5 s vs-Sword median with 6/12 sub-5 s finishes on re-measurement — the numbers initially recorded for this round came from an intermediate code state and are superseded by the measurements below):
  - Constant-torque detent: the lock is a series of clicks with roughly constant resisting torque τ = .5·k·releaseAngle (total barrier .5·k·releaseAngle², unchanged), replacing the quadratic energy well whose per-call work cap (remaining barrier energy) made accumulation asymptotic — traced locks stalled at 87–99% of release in every matchup.
  - Loading rate limit: at most 45% of the release angle per 0.5 s window (battle tick threaded through `resolveContact`/`applyRatchetWork`; direct unit callers omit the tick and keep per-call behaviour). A continuous contact calls the solver every tick, so per-call caps alone cannot distinguish one long clash from many strikes; a burst now needs band engagements spread over ~1 s or more. First-clash bursts are gone by construction (one window ≤ 45% < the 80% slip threshold).
  - Final-click slip: a strike pushing the lock past 80% of release completes it (a real pawl at high engagement slips the last clicks under vibration); traced peaks in aggressive matchups reach 84–99%.
  - Release angle lowered (.28+.09e+.06r → .22+.08e+.05r rad) and the angular-impulse coupling term carries a 2.5 factor with a .70 dissipation fraction — traced as the binding terms after the round-3 strike environment softened (launch speed × .92, restitution .30). `totalEnergy` now stores the detent potential as τ·lock, consistent with the linear model.
- Measured after all round-3 changes (same 12-seed protocol, clean tree): vs DranSword median 31.3 s (range 1.6–58.7, 3/12 under 5 s; 9 Spin / 1 Over / 1 Burst / 1 Xtreme); vs WizardArrow median 15.4 s (1.9–65.0, 2/12 under 5 s; 2 Bursts in the sample); mirror median 42.3 s (1.7–65.9, 3/12 under 5 s; 1 Burst). Bursts occur naturally (4 of 36) at non-instant times (earliest traced ~2.2 s). Solo spin (spin-down to |ω| < 5 rad/s from a .76 launch): Flat ~72 s (real 60–90 s ✓); Ball and Needle exceed the 400 s probe cap (real ~140–170 s / ~100–140 s), so the low-drag bits over-endure solo — a known limit left for a later round because raising their drag would shorten stamina battles that are already at the fast end. Still a tuned estimate, **not** a measured calibration and not real-world equivalence.
- Fixtures: the rail-bank Rush transfer fixture moved from angle .2 to .1 rad (at .2 the engagement is no longer powered under the new bowl; .1 satisfies every original assertion). Burst fixtures (unit, multiplayer, e2e) were reselected for the rate-limited detent: their selection lists now include seeded candidates measured bursting under the current physics (mirror seed 14 ~2.5 s, seed 8 ~4.3 s; e2e uses 76/−7°/12° with seed 9, ~2.2 s), selected deterministically in the running environment as before. No assertion was loosened.
- Presentation/customization shipped with this round: a staged 3-2-1-GO SHOOT! countdown overlay (wall-clock, driven by the existing launch deadline) and full opponent part selection (the CPU previously exposed only its blade).

## Round 3 follow-up: why stamina beys never met, and the center-dish fix (2026-10-31)

User report after round 3: beys still did not gather at the center, and stamina-vs-stamina rounds could run a whole round without contact. Measured diagnosis (Arrow/Ball and Arrow stock mirrors, 90 s probes): rounds with **zero collisions in 53 s** and first contact at 65 s — one top settled at the center while the other held a stable orbit at r ≈ .05–.18 m, just outside blade reach. A rest test isolated the mechanism: a spinning Ball/Needle top placed at rest at r = .06 m **did not move at all for 10 s**. The round-3 bowl spreads its 20 mm over the full 44 cm, so the slope at r = 5 cm is only ~2.4° — below the tip friction angle (Ball ≈ 8° at the then-current μ, Needle ≈ 5°) — and the contact solver's Coulomb friction can fully arrest slow drift each tick, so a slowed top freezes wherever it stops. Real BX-10 dimensions (Beyblade Wiki) explain the mismatch: battle zone Ø36.5 cm and the dish is a center feature inside the **tornado ridge Ø21 cm**, not a 44 cm-wide saucer.

Changes:
- **Center-dish boost**: the outer profile (r ≥ .13 m — rail, bank and pocket geometry) is bit-identical to round 3; inside r < .13 m a smooth boost (−K·(1−(r/a)²)², K = 10 mm, a = .13 m, C1 at the join) deepens the center and lifts the slope at r = 4–6 cm to ~6–8°, above the Ball/Needle friction angles. Rest test after: Ball placed at r = .06 m reaches r ≈ .01 m in ~10 s (Needle ~.005 m). Two earlier attempts were rejected on measurements: a full re-profile concentrated in the ridge circle raised the outer floor ~4 mm and produced launch-crossing rail ejections (5/7 stamina probes ended in ≤ 4 s) and a Hermite outer ease dug a moat at r ≈ .16–.18 where tops parked for 90 s.
- **Ball tip friction**: base .17 → .12 (a polished ball on smooth plastic scrubs continuously under precession, so the kinetic coefficient governs; derived Ball μ ≈ .14 → ≈ .095).
- **Rolling bleed** .004 → .006 (impulse-proportional coefficient): wide early orbits decay somewhat faster into the dish.
- **Detent window** 45% → 30% per 0.5 s and **final-click slip** 80% → 85%: with the deepened dish, stamina mirrors now clash head-on at the bottom at full early energy, and two max-budget windows burst a Ball build in ~2 s (traced). A burst now needs hard engagements across ~1.5 s or more.
- Rendering: the steeper center exposed a seam crack in the stadium tessellation (the polar grid's angle-0/2π columns are geometrically coincident but index-distinct, and refinement split only one twin). The mesher now marks every geometric twin of a marked edge; triangle count 25,044 → 24,912 with zero one-sided interior edges.
- Fixtures reselected under the new physics (assertions unchanged): three floor-contact unit tests now place tops relative to `bowlHeightAt` (the deepened floor invalidated absolute heights); burst candidate lists (unit + multiplayer) now lead with verified candidates (mirror seed 24 ~3.3 s, .9-power seed 7 ~7.8 s); the e2e burst fixture is 100/−20°/20° with seed 9 (player bursts ~2.9 s).

Measured after the follow-up (same protocols): stamina mirrors collide in **7 of 7** probe battles (first contact 0.5–10.4 s; repeated contacts, e.g. 11 collisions in a 53 s Arrow stock mirror). Distribution (12 seeds × 3 matchups): vs DranSword median 27.6 s (1/12 under 5 s, 0 bursts in sample), vs WizardArrow median 13.8 s (4/12 under 5 s, 1 burst), mirror median 26.5 s (3/12 under 5 s, 2 bursts). Solo spin: Flat 75 s (real 60–90 s ✓); Ball still exceeds the 400 s cap and Needle ~340 s (real ~140–170 / ~100–140 s) — the known solo over-endurance limit is unchanged. The stadium's total scale (floor radius .22 m, rail at .201 m) still reflects the product's outer dimensions rather than the 36.5 cm battle zone; rescaling the rail/pocket system is a separate, larger change left for a later round.

## Round 4: app-configuration audit, freak finishes, upright righting and model rebuild (2026-10-31)

User report after round 3 follow-up 2: rounds still far too short, model shapes "completely different", and beys still not gathering at the center. The audit found the previous rounds' probes had measured the wrong conditions: every distribution probe launched both tops at {.76, −7°, 6°}, but the app actually plays player defaults {power .60, angle 0°, tilt 20°} against a fixed CPU launch {.76, −.12 rad, .11 rad}, player Hells stock vs CPU DranSword stock. Re-measured in the app configuration, the shipped physics produced: default-matchup median 28.2 s but with freak finishes (seed 5: a 1.1 s double-exit draw — the CPU top rail-dashed at ~0.5 s, flew to y ≈ .18–.20 m and sailed out over the pockets while the player top was also airborne; seed 9: a 2.0 s "spin finish" where ONE collision flipped the loser to 94° tilt and cut its spin 354 → 74 rad/s), and stamina mirrors ending in 4–10 s bursts despite gathering at r ≈ .02–.03 — the center gathering worked geometrically, but rounds died there immediately, which is what the user saw as "not fixed".

Traced root causes and fixes:

## Component mass distribution and full inertia

The former vertical-only COM/scalar-inertia limitation is replaced by the [3D mass-distribution model](center-of-mass.md). Canonical geometry, positive estimated region densities and component masses produce the actual assembled COM and full symmetric tensor through volume moments and the parallel-axis theorem. Symmetric parts remain laterally centered; a lateral offset is never added merely to create a gameplay difference. Contacts, rendering, general rigid-body integration and Burst now consume the same component properties. These are estimated geometry/density results, not measured real-toy balance.
